Beyond the label

A label is not a mission.

We believe sustainability is a responsibility, not a certification.

We don't believe one label can tell the whole story. A certification can tell you where a material comes from, but not necessarily how long a product will last, how it was made, whether it can be repaired, or what happens when its life comes to an end.

Certifications have their place. But we've learned that a stamp of approval doesn't always mean a product is truly sustainable, and we believe sustainability should go much further than meeting a set of requirements.

We don't want to buy our way into being called sustainable. We want to earn it through the things we make. By questioning materials. By understanding their origins. By designing for longevity, repair and reuse. By taking responsibility for what happens long after a piece leaves our hands.

That's what the Passport is for: showing the evidence, piece by piece, instead of asking you to trust a stamp.

We don't claim to have all the answers. We're learning, questioning and challenging the way things have always been done.

Sustainability isn't a label you put on a product. It's a responsibility you build into it.

For hospitality

A story guests can read for themselves.

Hotels and restaurants are asked more and more what their spaces are made of, and furniture is often left out. The Passport is designed to give every piece a traceable origin, with something real behind it.

And it starts conversations. A guest scans a chair and reads that its wood once held up a family home in Java.
